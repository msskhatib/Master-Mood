From your door to orbit

ZOOM IN (1) | MOONSHOT AND EXAPTATION · STORY SPINE: Because of that: zoom in

Borrow the delivery-network logic, not the aircraft: a model that brings dinner today, adapted for orbital logistics, could help feed astronauts in 2041.

2026 · OBSERVED

Drones deliver food now. One delivery network passed 2 million commercial drone deliveries in January 2026, with a median flight of 3 minutes (Food On Demand). TRANSFER: scheduling, tracking, exception handling

2030s · PROPOSED TEST

Autonomy reaches orbit. Robotic freighters already berth at the station using its robotic arm. Next: test AI scheduling with qualified space partners. GATE: safety, communication delay, payload limits

2041 · MOONSHOT

Feeding the crew in space. Each astronaut needs about 1.7 kg of food a day; fresh produce arrives every three months (NASA). Imagine cargo and orbital crops supporting a lunar outpost. 10x AMBITION: on-demand, not occasional, resupply

E,O&T test. Start with a logistics simulation and a partner review, not launch infrastructure. For clients in logistics and food, the last mile becomes the last orbit.

The new gold

ZOOM IN (2) | TICK-TOCK AND ALL FOUR LENSES · STORY SPINE: Because of that: zoom in

TICK Gold is the treasure. TOCK Water, air, sand and plants are.

In 2041 value may sit in what keeps systems alive. Each card applies one of the 4 lenses of innovation and ends with an E,O&T opportunity.

And gold? Still useful: it coats the mirrors of the Webb space telescope. But in 2041 it no longer measures wealth.

Water

Training GPT-3 was estimated to evaporate about 700,000 litres of fresh water (Li et al.). The space station recycles 98% of its water (NASA). LEVERAGE RESOURCES: Adapt closed-loop thinking to a client water audit.

Sand

Sand and gravel use is about 50 billion tonnes a year, 17 kg per person per day (UNEP). Desert sand is too round for concrete. CHALLENGE ORTHODOXIES: Replace "abundant means disposable" with reuse by design.

Air

Lunar soil is 40 to 45% oxygen by weight (ESA), but it is chemically bound and not breathable until extracted. HARNESS TRENDS: Track extraction tests, energy needs and readiness.

Plants

Lettuce and chile peppers grow on the station (NASA): crop research, not yet a complete food system. UNDERSTAND NEEDS: Design for nutrition, reliable supply and the crew's experience.

How to read the treeLevel 1: worlds — Planning weights for the three worlds: 30%, 45%, 25%. They sum to 100%.

Level 2: one driver each — Three mutually exclusive outcomes per world, with conditional odds that sum to 100% within that world.

Joint odds — World x outcome. Worked example: Patchwork and partly compatible = 45% x 40% = 18%. All nine joint odds total 100%.

Why Patchwork leads — AI rules are already splitting by region; the clean-power race is not yet won.

DECISION PASSPORT

WORKED EXAMPLE: OMAR'S DISPATCH IN OVERHEAT (ILLUSTRATIVE REHEARSAL)

DECISION

May the AI dispatch this van, or must Omar use the verified manual route?

ASSUMPTION

Route and charging data are current enough for this dispatch.

EVIDENCE

Time-stamped sources, a human route check and pilot results, still to be collected.

OWNER

Omar authorises; dispatchers can flag an alert.

REVIEW SIGNAL

Conflicting or untraceable alerts, or no charging available.

FALLBACK

Verify by phone, use the manual route and tell the customer.

Proposed service concepts and an illustrative rehearsal, not existing products or results.

Prove it is useful before scaling it

FIRST 90 DAYS | SMALL TESTS, CLEAR OWNERS · STORY SPINE: Ever since: first test

One client, one workflow, one limited rehearsal. All activities and gates below are proposed; no client commitment or benefit is claimed.

01

DAYS 1 TO 30 — Define and observe

OWNERS Client owner and consultant. Map one workflow, record a manual baseline and interview five users. GATE: agree success, error severity, data permissions and limits.

02

DAYS 31 TO 60 — Prototype and rehearse

OWNERS Technical lead and client users. Build a simulation and a Decision Passport; rehearse all three worlds. GATE: five users can name the evidence, owner and fallback.

03

DAYS 61 TO 90 — Stress-test and decide

OWNERS Sponsor and independent reviewer. Run ten scripted cases: low power, false input, provider outage and normal operation. GATE: every critical fallback works; no unresolved material error.

Continue, change or stop

Scale only when the pilot shows client-valued benefit and acceptable resource use.

Pilot limit. Five users and ten cases support learning, not proof of general reliability.

REHEARSE | 20 MIN — Learn through the scenario

OCTALYSIS, USED ETHICALLY. Pick a world and a role. A shared progress bar tracks evidence, owner and fallback; retries are allowed after feedback.

AI AND SCIENCE FICTION. Try one strange AI or sci-fi variation as an idea, not a fact. Check its assumptions and keep a testable version in the commonplace book.

Ethical design. Reward learning and feedback, not speed or a public rank. No penalties for failed ideas.

What I am taking with me

PART 2 | PERSONAL REFLECTION | 1 OF 2

The tools that resonated, the change in my thinking, and why it matters in my professional life.

Three ideas from this course changed how I think about planning: possibility trees, working backwards, and counterfactual thinking. Together they gave me a structured language for something my work has always demanded: decisions about a future nobody can see.

In twenty years of network engineering and digital transformation, I have learned that a platform chosen today may still shape a business in ten years. Building the three worlds of 2041 for this assignment showed me a different way to approach that kind of decision. Drawing the possibility tree forced me to give each branch a probability, and the less likely branches raised questions a single forecast would never have asked.

Working backwards from a 2041 press release, the way Amazon writes the press release before the product exists, made the first steps for 2026 obvious. Counterfactual thinking kept the exercise honest: writing Overheat next to Symbiosis stopped me from falling in love with the optimistic future.

How I will put it into practice

PART 2 | PERSONAL REFLECTION | 2 OF 2

A realistic timetable, a review loop and a personal routine that continues beyond this course.

My plan is concrete. From November, every new technical proposal I write will open with a one-page working backwards brief describing the result for the customer in three years, the assumptions behind it and the first test.

Once a quarter, starting in January 2027, I will build a possibility tree for one roadmap decision, with three branches, nine sub-branches and probabilities, and review it three months later to see which branch reality followed. Before each major design review I will run one upward and one downward counterfactual with the team and record the risks it surfaces.

Personally, I live between Riyadh and Stockholm, and I will turn those flights into fuzzy focus time: no screens, just my stimuli queue and a commonplace book, so slow hunches have somewhere to land.
