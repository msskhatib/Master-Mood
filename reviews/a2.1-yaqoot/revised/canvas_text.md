[cell] Our Customers | Who are our target customers? | People who want to run their phone line from an app, and visitors who need a Saudi number on arrival. Defined by the job, not by age or income. | Resident who lives on the phone: run my mobile life from the app, pay only for what I use in each 4-week cycle, never visit a shop. | Pains: must pick among 13 voice packages (SAR 67 to 385), about 65 plans in all; overpays or runs out; keeps a second SIM as insurance (37% multi-SIM on the Zain network); app faults behind the 4.3 rating. | Visitor who has just landed (29.3m in 2025, 18.03m for Umrah): be reachable on a Saudi number before leaving the airport. | Pains: kiosk queue plus 10 to 15 minutes at the counter, or about 20 minutes of app steps on roaming data (A).

[cell] Our Capabilities | What are our top capabilities that will make us more likely to succeed than the competition? | We run on our group's own network and are the challenger, so a fair-bill promise is easier for us than for rivals. | Unfair advantage (VRIO): the only app-only brand on a network its group owns (11,446 sites), inside a 14.6% challenger with most to gain from being the primary line. Hard to copy for brands on rented networks; costlier for stc (49.0% base). To be confirmed by the pilot. | Temporary lead: visitor verification through Absher since 2025, first in the Kingdom. | Above its national share in Makkah and Madinah. | Partly used: Tamam (licensed 2021, SAR 435.3m revenue in 2025) is a payment option at checkout. | Test not yet passed: Organised. The BSS must reprice and settle credits (upgrade announced April 2026) and Finance must approve the economics.

[cell] What resources will we need? | A small protected team, billing changes, three partners and a sponsor. | Team of 11 for 18 months (A). | Cycle re-pricing and a recommendation model on the BSS being upgraded with Huawei. | Agreements: Ministry of Interior (Absher), Nusuk and travel platforms, Tamam. | Peak-season capacity in Makkah, Madinah and airports; in-app care with issue status. | Sponsor: head of consumer. About SAR 30 to 35m (A), with enrolment paused at 3% credit exposure. | What risks may we encounter along the way? And how will we mitigate them? | The promise may cost revenue, hurt the main brand, or be copied or diluted. | R1 Guarantee cuts revenue (H/H): Fit offered at random to 10% of prospects, against control; pause enrolment before credits reach 3% of pilot revenue; honour earned credits. | R2 Takes customers from the main Zain brand (H/M): position by job; expand only if group contribution is non-negative. | R3 stc copies the Absher journey or cuts Jawwy's price (M/H): keep the promise; reassess if matched. | R4 Clan culture dilutes the promise (M/H): sponsor signs it in one sentence; changes only at gates. | R5 Absher or consent rules change (L/H): alternative route or refund; separate consent. | R6 BSS upgrade slips (M/M): start with recommendations; manual credits only in an audited pilot. | R7 Network below parity in 9 metros (M/H): seasonal capacity; first cycle refunded. | How will we know we are heading in the right direction? | Results for customers and the business, not tasks completed. | Primary-line share: 50% (A) to 65% by month 18. | Customers kept after 12 cycles: 55% (A) to 70% by month 18. | Yaqoot revenue growth: 1% (H1 2025) to 10% by month 18. | Visitor activation: median 5 minutes or less from verification start, 90% within 30 minutes, by month 12 (baseline measured before pilot). | Google Play rating: 4.3 to 4.6 by month 18. | Gate 1: retention 3 points above control, revenue at 97% or more.

[cell] Our Product Vision | Yaqoot is the line that fits itself to you. Residents make it their primary line because the price stays fair as their needs change, and visitors have a working Saudi number within minutes of arriving. | What will the world look like for us if our product is successful? | Yaqoot becomes the main line, not the spare one, and grows again. | Primary line for 65% of resident customers (about 50% today, A). | Revenue grows 10% a year again (1% in H1 2025), from former second-SIM holders and visitors. | 70% of new residents stay beyond 12 cycles. | Known for the best-plan guarantee, a stronger position than a price cut. | Yaqoot, network and Tamam work from one plan with one sponsor.

[cell] Our Competitors | Who are our main competitors? | Other app-only brands, visitor SIMs, the airport kiosk and the habit of a second SIM. | Jawwy (stc group, 49.0% share): app-only, from SAR 34.50 a month. | Virgin Mobile (6.2%, on stc network): eSIM-native, from SAR 51.75. Lebara (4.8%, on Mobily): international calling, CX award 2025. | Salam (0.8%, on Zain's network) and the main Zain app (digital packages SAR 200 to 230): overlap in-house. | Visitors: stc Sawa Visitor from SAR 31, Mobily Visitor from SAR 34.50 (Yaqoot: SAR 34.50 for 10 days), airport kiosks, data-only travel eSIMs. | Category: virtual operators hold 7.4m lines (12%); Yaqoot growth fell from 13% (2024) to 1% (H1 2025). | Gap to own: no rival offers a plan that corrects itself.

[cell] Our Culture | Are we a clan, an adhocracy, a market or a hierarchy? | A Clan: a family-like culture that cooperates well but decides slowly. Second leaning: Adhocracy. | Clan evidence: Belonging value (one family, second home), BE WELL and UNITY programmes, Forbes no. 1 telecom employer, 44% women in executive roles. | Adhocracy evidence: Agility value, ZANIAC ideas programme, Yaqoot (2018) and Tamam (2019) built in-house. | Helps: Yaqoot, network and Tamam teams cooperate easily. | Hinders: the wish for agreement may dilute the guarantee and delay the decision to take customers from the main brand.
