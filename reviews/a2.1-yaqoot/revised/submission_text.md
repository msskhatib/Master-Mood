So the product is a service. The app is how the customer meets it, the package is what the customer buys, and the engine and the network deliver it. The only physical items are the SIM card, when the customer does not use eSIM, and the devices sold in the app \[2\]. Yaqoot Fit develops the app, the package rules and the engine, and leaves the network technology as it is. Seen through Market, Implementation and Technology, most of the change is in implementation: how the bill is priced and when the visitor is brought on board. The technology exists (eSIM, Nafath, Absher, 5G) or is already planned (the BSS upgrade), which keeps the risk in a place the team controls.

**The technology in plain words.** Four pieces of technology make the product and the proposal possible.

> • **eSIM.** A SIM that is downloaded, not posted. It removes the delivery wait. Visitors can reserve before flying; eSIM download before travel depends on validation (2.3).
>
> • **Digital identity.** Every Saudi line must be tied to a verified person. Nafath does this for residents and Absher for visitors, from the phone \[1\], \[2\].
>
> • **BSS.** The system that knows each customer\'s package, counts usage and takes payment. A bill can only correct itself if this system can price a finished cycle again against the other eligible plans. The upgrade announced in April 2026 is the enabler \[5\], \[25\].
>
> • **Usage data.** The same system records what each customer used in each cycle. This is the input for a plan recommendation.

## **2.2 Where the product falls short today**

Its revenue grew 13% in 2024 \[31\] and only 1% year on year in the first and second quarters of 2025 \[32\], \[33\], the last periods for which a figure is published. ## **2.3 The development: Yaqoot Fit, the line that fits itself**

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **In plain words.** After every four weeks, Yaqoot checks what you actually used. If a cheaper package would have covered it, the difference comes back to you as credit. Example: a customer on the SAR 125 package (50 GB) who used 25 GB and stayed within 300 calls and texts would have been covered by the SAR 90 package (30 GB), so SAR 35 comes back as credit. The customer no longer has to guess.
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The proposal has three parts. Each answers a gap in 2.2 and develops one or more of the four parts in 2.1. Figure 5 shows how each part could look in the app, and section 5.1 shows the order in which they are built.

> 1\. **Fit (addresses package choice and second-line use).** After each completed four-week cycle, Yaqoot compares the customer\'s usage with the published set of eligible plans, including data, calls and app allowances. If a cheaper eligible plan would have covered that use, the difference is issued as a credit for the next cycle. There is never a retrospective surcharge. Before enrolment, Yaqoot publishes how earned credits are settled for customers who leave before renewal, and suspending new enrolment does not cancel earned credits. After two cycles above the allowance, the app proposes the next plan up. This is a best-plan guarantee.
>
> 2\. **Arrive (answers the visitor gap).** The visitor starts before travelling, with a reservation and instructions. Payment and eSIM download before travel are switched on once the supported identity, provisioning and refund paths are confirmed. On arrival the visitor completes the eligible verification flow, and the aim is a working Saudi number within five minutes of starting verification. Visitor packages already exist for 10, 14, 21 and 28 days from SAR 34.50 \[2\].
>
> 3\. **Stay (answers the stalled growth).** One account for the main line, family lines and the home package, building on the September 2025 multi-number feature \[4\]. Pay with Tamam already exists at checkout \[2\]. Stay tests device instalments in the same account, as an option for residents who already buy devices at checkout, with Tamam, licensed by the Saudi Central Bank in 2021 \[29\], and only after separate consent.

## **3.2 Category: where do we compete and what is changing?**

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **In plain words.** Yaqoot sells mobile lines that are bought and managed in an app. That used to be special. Now several brands do it, many people carry two SIM cards, and millions of visitors arrive every year. Yaqoot\'s own growth slowed in the last published period.
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A category is the mental filing cabinet a customer uses to understand what a product is. Customers file Yaqoot under app-only mobile lines, next to Jawwy and Virgin Mobile. That category has no clear leader of its own: the leader of the wider mobile category, stc, is also behind Jawwy. Five trends shape the category.

> 1\. **The market is concentrated.** stc holds 49.0%, Mobily 23.8% and Zain KSA 14.6%. Virgin Mobile holds 6.2%, Lebara 4.8%, Salam 0.8% and Red Bull Mobile 0.8% \[11\].
>
> 2\. **The app-only idea has spread.** stc\'s sub-brand Jawwy moved to a new digital platform in 2025 \[14\]. Virgin Mobile sells eSIM-native plans and entertainment bundles \[10\].
>
> 3\. **Virtual operators are growing.** These are brands that rent a network instead of owning one. They hold about 7.4 million lines, around 12% of the market \[10\].
>
> 4\. **Second SIMs are the exposure.** Multi-SIM use is 17% on stc, 27% on Mobily and 37% on Zain. Opensignal names migration to virtual operators and secondary SIMs as Zain\'s main exposure \[10\].
>
> 5\. **Visitors, eSIM and official apps are rising together.** 29.3 million international visitors in 2025 \[15\]. The Nusuk app has more than 51 million users \[17\]. The Saudi eSIM market is forecast to grow about 12% a year to 2034 \[24\].

![](media/image8.png){width="6.377952755905512in" height="1.9282185039370079in"}

Figure 6. Market share, multi-SIM use and Yaqoot revenue growth. Data: Argaam \[11\], Opensignal \[10\], Zain KSA \[31\] and Zain Group \[32\], \[33\].

The conclusion for strategy: being digital is now the entry ticket, and Yaqoot\'s slowdown from 13% to 1% growth fits that. The open question in the category is who becomes the customer\'s main line. Categories evolve, and this is the opening for Yaqoot: instead of fighting for second place in app-only lines, it can define a new drawer in the filing cabinet, the line that fits itself, and be its first name.

## **3.4 Our Capabilities: what makes us more likely to succeed than the competition?**

## **3.5 Our Culture: are we a clan, an adhocracy, a market or a hierarchy?**

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **In plain words.** The company behaves like a family: people, belonging and wellbeing come first, with some appetite for new ideas. That helps teams work together. It can also slow down hard decisions, because everyone wants agreement.
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Competing Values Framework sorts cultures by two tensions: flexibility against control, and internal against external focus \[27\]. From public evidence, Zain KSA reads as a **Clan** culture with a second leaning to **Adhocracy**. Four of six pieces of evidence point to Clan. The reading is of the dominant type, not the only one: results statements also stress disciplined execution and governance \[6\], \[7\], a Hierarchy trait that any listed, regulated operator shows. I weigh it below Clan because it appears in financial reporting, while the Clan evidence appears in the values, the programmes and the awards the company chooses to lead with.

# **4. Future State**

## **4.1 Our Product Vision**

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Yaqoot is the line that fits itself to you. Residents make it their primary line because the price stays fair as their needs change, and visitors have a working Saudi number within minutes of arriving.**
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In the Geoffrey Moore format: for people who run their mobile life from their phone, who are tired of choosing among dozens of plans and carrying a second SIM, Yaqoot is an all-digital mobile service that credits the difference whenever a cheaper plan would have covered the cycle. Unlike Jawwy, Virgin Mobile and Lebara, which sell fixed bundles, Yaqoot guarantees the best plan and runs on a network its group owns.

The vision is the North Star for every choice in the Plan. It answers why before what: people should not have to manage their mobile plan at all. It is clear (two sentences a customer could repeat), aspirational (the provider carries the risk of a wrong plan, which reverses how mobile is sold) and names both impacts: for the customer, a price that stays fair and a number within minutes; for the business, becoming the primary line.

# **7. Conclusion**

Yaqoot was early with an idea the market has since copied, and its growth fell from 13% to 1%. The context shows where the next advantage lies: 37% of devices on this network carry a second SIM, 29.3 million visitors arrive each year, and Yaqoot is the one app-only brand that runs on its group\'s own network and has the most to gain from becoming the main line. The Future State is a line that fits itself and that residents choose as their primary line. The Plan reaches it in three gated phases, tests the economics in a controlled pilot before expanding, names the risks that come from stc, from the company\'s own main brand and from its culture, and measures success by customers kept, primary-line share and revenue growth, not by features shipped.
